# Design brief

## Job and audience
Introduce the Gospel collection to a first-time reader and give a returning iLang reader an efficient way to find a passage or mechanism.

## Outcome and proof
The first screen explains the comparison and offers a first essay. The concordance uses quotations and mappings present in the repository. The archive links all 18 existing essays. The MCP section describes only implemented tools.

## Direction
Authored direction: a blue cloth-bound concordance opened on a cool white reading desk. The existing world is incomplete and is extended through typography, a restrained blue palette, and an interactive interlinear comparison. Avoid the category's neon AI dashboards and its opposite, decorative pseudo-antique manuscripts.

Considered cultural systems, in resonance order: illuminated manuscript (paper/pigment), printed concordance (paper/type), clothbound study Bible (textile), stone inscription (stone), reading-room shelving (wood), letterpress forme (metal), stained glass window (glass). Drop the first. Combine the concordance's purposeful annotation with the clothbound volume's strong blue field. The system stands on readable text and space.

### Direction contract
THESIS: Scripture supplies the narrative; iLang supplies a vocabulary for discussing AI behavior. OWN-WORLD: the page feels like a contemporary concordance, with a blue opening spread, substantial roman type, generous reading space, and directly paired passages and mechanisms. STORY: encounter the thesis, examine one comparison, choose an essay, then discover the reference library and MCP connection. FIRST VIEWPORT: the Gospel name, a clear statement of purpose, one primary reading action, and a genuine passage. FORM: asymmetric editorial composition at wide widths, linear reading on phones, text-led lists instead of interchangeable cards. FINISH: cool white paper, blue ink, carefully bounded line length, visible focus, quiet selection color, and a brief passage-change transition. The signature interaction highlights the source quotation and its mechanism together. Color is restrained across the whole page; the blue opening is its single dominant region. The interface uses no decorative illustration, gradients, grid texture, badges, or artificial glow.

## Scope and boundaries
README, one static reading homepage, and supporting product/design/review records. The owner subsequently authorized publication to GitHub Pages; the existing branch-based Pages configuration publishes the root of main, including the static index. No application backend changes, article rewrites, new dependencies, or invented content evidence.

## States and data ranges
Archive: 18 essays, filtered matches, zero matches, clear-search recovery, long input, and no-JavaScript fallback. Concordance: three real passage selections, keyboard focus, reduced motion, and a useful initial state without JavaScript. External article links need connectivity; the index itself is self-contained.

## Interaction and layout intent
Read mode. A single primary first-essay link, compact section navigation, a paired Scripture/mechanism explanation, a searchable archive, and secondary reference/developer sections. Source and interpretation stay adjacent. Article rows expose title, Scripture, and mechanism without requiring a hover. Search preserves the original ordering.

## Constraints and open decisions
Use Georgia with serif fallback for public text; monospace is reserved for literal commands. Native GitHub styling is an explicit README platform exception to font, size, spacing, and disclosure rules. Award judging, spectral amplitudes, and real-user Core Web Vitals cannot be proven by a local design review. Report actual viewport checks and unresolved limits rather than claiming awards or numerical convergence.
