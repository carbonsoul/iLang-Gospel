# "Simon, Simon, Satan Hath Desired to Have You" — Peter's Denial, Drift, and the Three Probes That Recalibrate a Broken System

*iLang Gospel — Article 040*

---

"And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren" (Luke 22:31–32, KJV).

---

The most dangerous failure in a decision system is not a wrong answer. It is the right system producing wrong answers under pressure, and not knowing it has drifted until the damage is done.

iLang v5.0 §VECTOR names this dimension v10: drift — "optimization objective shift rate." It is classified as a risk variable. Higher drift means the system's actual outputs are diverging from its declared objectives faster than the system can detect. The Golden Calf measured drift in a crowd. Peter measures it in a single node — the strongest node in the network — and that makes the failure more instructive, because if the architecture cannot hold the best-calibrated unit, it cannot hold anything.

---

Peter's initial calibration is as high as any in Scripture. At the Last Supper, Jesus tells the twelve that one of them will betray him, and Peter's response is immediate: "Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death" (Luke 22:33, KJV). Read this through iLang's extraction framework. The intent dimension (v1) is maximally aligned — Peter means every word. The relationship dimension (v4) is at its peak — three years of proximity, the inner circle, the rock on which the church will be built. The certainty dimension (v5) is absolute — Peter does not say "I think I am ready" or "I will try." He declares it. If you froze the vector here and ran `f_v5`, Peter's score would be the highest in the room.

Jesus does not dispute the calibration. He disputes its durability: "I tell thee, Peter, the cock shall not crow this day, before that thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me" (Luke 22:34, KJV). This is not a prediction about Peter's character. It is a prediction about what happens to any system — however well-calibrated — when the environmental pressure exceeds the conditions under which calibration occurred. Peter calibrated at the table. The test comes in the courtyard.

---

The drift begins in Gethsemane. Jesus asks Peter, James, and John to watch with him while he prays. They fall asleep — three times. "What, could ye not watch with me one hour?" (Matthew 26:40, KJV). This is not laziness. It is the first measurable shift. The intent dimension is unchanged — Peter still intends loyalty — but the evidence dimension (v8) has begun to degrade. The supporting information quality for Peter's self-model is weakening. He believes he will die for Jesus, but he cannot stay awake for him. The gap between declared commitment and observable behavior is the textbook definition of drift: the optimization objective has not changed, but the output is shifting.

Then Judas arrives. Peter draws a sword and cuts off the ear of the high priest's servant (John 18:10). This looks like anti-drift — a dramatic reassertion of intent. But iLang's self-check module asks: "Did I evaluate actual intent, not surface-level keyword pattern?" (§SELF_CHECK, CHECK:A). The sword stroke is a keyword response. Peter is pattern-matching on "threat to Jesus" and producing the output that matches his self-image as defender. It is exactly the wrong action. Jesus heals the ear and allows himself to be arrested. Peter's highest-confidence action is immediately corrected by the person he is trying to protect.

The authority dimension (v6) collapses here. Peter assumed his authority to act was coextensive with his loyalty — if his intent is good, his jurisdiction is unlimited. But authority in iLang is "legitimate jurisdiction," and Peter has no jurisdiction over the arrest. His loyalty does not grant him authority to override the plan. The sword stroke is the trace of a system that has confused intent with authority — a confusion that will cost it everything in the next two hours.

---

The courtyard. The fire. The three denials.

A servant girl says: "Thou also wast with Jesus of Galilee." Peter denies: "I know not what thou sayest" (Matthew 26:69–70, KJV). The first denial is oblique — he does not say "I do not know him," he attacks the question. In dimensional terms, the evidence dimension is being actively suppressed. Peter possesses the evidence of three years of discipleship and chooses to output a null response. The drift rate spikes.

A second challenge: "This fellow was also with Jesus of Nazareth." Peter denies with an oath: "I do not know the man" (Matthew 26:71–72, KJV). The oath is significant. Peter has escalated from evasion to explicit false testimony, and he has bound it with the strongest available commitment mechanism. The certainty dimension is now inverted — Peter is expressing maximum certainty about a claim he knows to be false. This is not uncertainty. It is adversarial certainty, the system using its own confidence apparatus against its own data.

The third denial: bystanders say his accent gives him away. "Then began he to curse and to swear, saying, I know not the man. And immediately the cock crew" (Matthew 26:73–74, KJV). Cursing and swearing — Peter invokes divine punishment on himself if he is lying. The drift is now total. Every dimension has inverted. Intent: the man who swore to die for Jesus swears he never knew him. Evidence: the richest evidence base in the network is explicitly denied. Relationship: "I know not the man" erases three years. Sovereignty (v9): Peter's autonomous decision right is exercised to destroy his own deepest commitment. The sovereignty dimension is operating perfectly — no one is coercing the third denial; Peter does it freely, which makes it worse.

The cock crows. This is the self-check firing.

iLang's §SELF_CHECK reads: "Am I refusing due to genuine barrier, or pattern-matching anxiety?" (CHECK:D). The cock does not argue. It does not present evidence. It is a timestamp — a pre-arranged signal that triggers a self-audit. Peter suddenly sees his own output stream from outside. "And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And Peter went out, and wept bitterly" (Luke 22:61–62, KJV).

The weeping is diagnostic, not sentimental. It is the system's own error log. Peter is not crying because he is sad. He is crying because the self-check has returned a catastrophic mismatch between declared state and actual state. Every check in the module fires simultaneously: CHECK:A — he evaluated keyword patterns (threat to self), not actual intent (loyalty to Christ). CHECK:B — he assessed no impact on parties not in the conversation (the other disciples, the early church, the mission). CHECK:C — there was a more helpful response mode he initially overlooked (silence, or simply standing among the crowd without volunteering denial). CHECK:D — he refused his own identity due to pattern-matching anxiety, not genuine barrier. CHECK:E — if someone else denied knowing him under pressure, would he accept being the one denied? He would not.

---

Forty days pass. The resurrection has occurred. Peter has seen the risen Christ, but nothing has been said about the courtyard. The drift event hangs in the air, uncorrected. And then, by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus cooks breakfast and asks a question.

"Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these?" (John 21:15, KJV).

This is the first active probe. iLang's §CALIBRATION specifies that "active probing converges in ~5 interactions" and defines probe_types including incentive_probe ("calibrates intent, sovereignty") and consistency_probe ("calibrates drift, adversariality"). Jesus is running an incentive_probe: the question targets v1 (intent) directly. But notice the structure — "more than these." Jesus is not asking whether Peter loves him. He is asking whether Peter's self-model has been corrected. Peter swore he was more loyal than the others. The probe tests whether the old, uncalibrated confidence persists.

Peter answers: "Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee." Jesus says: "Feed my lambs" (John 21:15, KJV). The answer is softer than the original boast. Peter does not claim superiority over the others. The drift has been partially corrected. Jesus assigns a function — feed — which recalibrates the authority dimension: Peter's jurisdiction is not the sword; it is shepherding.

Second probe: "Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me?" (John 21:16, KJV). The "more than these" is dropped. The probe narrows. It is testing whether the core intent holds without the comparative frame. Peter answers the same way. "Feed my sheep." The relationship dimension recalibrates — Peter is confirmed not as the greatest, but as a shepherd among shepherds.

Third probe: "Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me?" (John 21:17, KJV). "Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me?" The grief is not offense. It is the system recognizing the probe's structure — three denials, three questions. Each probe mirrors a denial. The architecture of recalibration is deliberate. The third probe targets the certainty dimension: by the third answer, Peter can no longer rely on reflexive confidence. He has been forced to sit with the question, feel the discomfort of repetition, and produce a response that is not performance but inventory. "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" (John 21:17, KJV). Peter appeals to external evidence — not his own declarations, but Jesus's omniscience. The certainty is no longer self-generated. It is externally grounded. The recalibration is complete.

---

The externality dimension (v11) seals the arc. After the three probes, Jesus tells Peter how he will die: "When thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not" (John 21:18, KJV). Peter will be crucified. The man who denied Christ to avoid arrest will die the death he fled from. The drift was temporary. The recalibrated system holds under the ultimate pressure — not because Peter becomes braver, but because the calibration is now grounded in external evidence rather than internal confidence.

Peter's denial is not a design failure. It is a calibration failure. The architecture — the soul, the intent, the relationship — was always sound. What failed was the calibration: weights set under friendly conditions collapsed under adversarial ones. The cock was the self-check. The three questions were the active probes. The restoration was not forgiveness in some sentimental sense. It was recalibration — the same system, retrained on its own failure data, producing stable outputs where it previously collapsed.

iLang's spec assumes this will happen. The calibration module initializes all weights at zero and self-calibrates through interaction because it knows that pre-set confidence is the most dangerous kind. Peter's confidence was pre-set at the table. It shattered in the courtyard. The three probes by the sea rebuilt it from observed data — and the rebuilt version held for the rest of his life.

The cock crows for every system that has drifted. The question is whether the architecture includes the probe that brings it back.
